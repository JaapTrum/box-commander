Box Commander

A grab-and-play multiplayer Commander variant for 3 to 6 players. One box, shared piles, real commanders, and a secret way to win.

Overview Rules Setup Commanders Ambitions

Overview

The idea, and how the pieces fit together.

Box Commander is Commander in a box. Everything you need is in there: seven shared piles of 100 cards (one per color, plus colorless and gold), a stack of real legendary commanders, and a stack of secret ambition cards. Pick a commander, draw from the piles your commander's colors allow, and play. Setup takes a couple of minutes and nobody has to build a deck.

Every game plays differently. The piles roll on from one game to the next instead of being reshuffled, every table gets a different mix of commanders, and each player chases their own secret ambition alongside the usual fight to be the last one standing.

Shared piles

Five color piles, one colorless pile, and one gold pile of multicolor cards. You draw from the piles inside your commander's color identity, plus colorless and gold. Nobody owns a pile; everyone in your colors draws from the same one.

Real commanders

Each player is dealt three real legendary creatures and picks one. Fewer colors means a stronger commander, more colors means more pile choice and a weaker card.

Secret ambitions

Each player is dealt two ambition cards and keeps one face down. An ambition is a hidden, hard alternate win condition that you have to set up and pull off.

The goal: a ready-to-go, easy-to-understand, different-every-time game for 3 to 6 players that stays balanced and fun. No deck building, no shuffling in play, no mana screw or flood, and a distinct plan for every commander.

Rules

Standard Commander rules apply unless noted here.

The piles

The box holds seven piles of 100 cards each. Piles are never shuffled during a game, and the box contains no search or shuffle effects.

The Gold pile

The Gold pile holds cards with an obvious multicolor identity, from two colors up to five. Anyone may draw from it, but every draw is a gamble: a gold card only helps you if it shares at least one color with your commander's color identity.

  • Look in secret. When you draw a gold card, look at it without showing anyone. If it shares at least one color with your commander's color identity, you keep it.
  • Luck of the draw. You cannot redraw a card that matches. You may only redraw if you can prove the card does not match: show it to the table, put it on the bottom of the Gold pile, and draw again from the Gold pile.
  • You lose the gamble. You may redraw at most twice per draw. If the third card is also a mismatch, you keep it as a dead card.
  • No cheating. You never have to show a card you keep, but if you cast a gold card that shares no color with your commander, the whole table will know.
  • Flashback costs are ignored when checking a card's colors, and so are other costs outside the mana cost.
  • Mostly-one-color cards with a small second color elsewhere on the card live in their main color's pile and have no restriction. Only cards that are clearly multicolor are in the Gold pile.
Why it works: a mono-color commander matches fewer gold cards, so the Gold pile is a poorer bet for the strongest commanders. A five-color commander matches every gold card.

Drawing and mana

  • Draw step: instead of drawing from a library, choose one option. Take the top card of any pile you are allowed to draw from, take one basic land from the supply, or take the top card of a land stack you are allowed to draw from.
  • Basic lands from the supply: take any basic you like, including Wastes. Basics are not any-color, so think about which one you take.
  • Land stacks: each land box also holds 5 non-basic lands of that color, kept face down as a stack. You may take the top card of a color's stack if that color is in your commander's color identity. You cannot look first. The Colorless stack is open to everyone.
  • Land drops work as normal: one per turn.
  • No mulligans. Your opening hand is fixed (see Setup).
  • Empty pile: if the pile you want is empty, draw from another allowed pile or take a basic. When every pile you can draw from is empty, you can only take basics.

Commanders

  • Standard Commander rules: cast your commander from the command zone, commander tax of {2} per previous cast, 40 starting life, and 21 commander damage eliminates a player.
  • Color identity only decides which piles you can draw from. It does not restrict the cards you hold or the basics you take.
  • Each player has their own graveyard. Graveyards are never shared, and this matters for commanders like Muldrotha.

No shuffling, rolling piles

  • Never shuffle a pile during the game. Cards that go to a graveyard or exile stay there until the game ends.
  • After the game, sort used cards back into their color's used bin. The next game continues from where each pile left off.
  • When a pile runs low, reset it with a pile deal: deal its used cards into 4 to 6 stacks, then stack those in a random order. Do this between games, not during one.
  • The small commander and ambition stacks can be shuffled normally.

Ambitions

Each player has one secret ambition, kept face down. There are three types:

Kill: when you eliminate a player, if the condition is true, you win the game.

Showdown: when you would lose the game and exactly two players remain, if the condition is true, you win instead.

Defeat: when you would lose the game at any point, if the condition is true, you win instead.

  • "Eliminate" means a loss caused by something you controlled: combat damage, commander damage, or a spell or ability.
  • Kill ambitions are checked right after the elimination resolves. Showdown and Defeat ambitions are checked at the moment you would lose.
  • If several ambitions trigger at once, resolve them in turn order. Reveal your ambition when it wins or when the game ends.
  • Ambitions are an extra way to win, not a replacement. If nobody completes their ambition, the last player standing wins the conventional way.

Optional rules

  • Bail-out: at any time, reveal and discard your ambition to draw two cards.
  • Callout: once per game, name another player's ambition. If you are right, they discard it.
  • Winner's tax: whoever won the previous game starts last in turn order.

Setup

From box to first turn in a few minutes. Works with 3 to 6 players.

  1. Lay out the piles. Place the seven piles face down in the middle of the table, plus the land boxes: 20 basics of each type and 15 Wastes, with a face-down stack of 5 non-basic lands in each color box and in the Colorless box.
  2. Deal commanders and ambitions. Shuffle the commander stack and the ambition stack. Deal each player 3 commanders face up and 2 ambitions face down, all at the same time. In a 3-player game, take Rampage out of the ambition stack first.
  3. Pick a pair that works together. Look at all five cards, then choose 1 commander and 1 ambition that fit each other. Keep your ambition face down and return the unused cards to the box.
  4. Set your life. Everyone starts at 40 life. Put your commander in the command zone.
  5. Take your opening hand. Take 3 basics of your choice from the supply. Then draw 4 cards, one at a time, from piles and land stacks you are allowed to draw from.
  6. Pick a starting player. Roll a die or use last game's rule if you are using the winner's tax. Play proceeds clockwise.
  7. Play. On your draw step, draw from an allowed pile or take a basic. First elimination or last two players is where ambitions come alive.
After the game: return commanders and ambitions to their stacks, send used cards back to their color's used bin, and keep the piles in their current order for next time.

Commanders

28 real legendary creatures. Images load from Scryfall, so you need an internet connection for artwork.

Power is tuned by color count. Mono-color commanders are the strongest cards in the stack, two-color are mid-power, three-color are weaker, and five-color are the weakest of all. The more colors you have, the more piles you can draw from, which makes up the difference.

10 commanders, strongest cards

10 commanders, mid-power

Three-color

5 commanders, weaker cards, more piles

Five-color

2 commanders, weakest cards, all piles

Ambitions

30 secret alternate win conditions. Each player is dealt 2 and keeps 1.

An ambition is a win condition you have to execute on. It adds a layer of strategy on top of the normal game, and because it is secret, the rest of the table has to guess what you are up to. If nobody completes their ambition, the last player standing wins the conventional way.
